## Short Answer

Insurance claims are denied for reasons including policy exclusions, missed deadlines, insufficient documentation, disputed liability, and pre-existing conditions. The denial letter must explain the reason, and most denials can be appealed.

## Common Reason 1: Policy Exclusion

Your policy may not cover the type of loss you are claiming. For example, standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood damage. Auto policies may exclude certain uses of the vehicle. Review your policy to understand what is and is not covered before assuming the denial is wrongful.

## Common Reason 2: Lapsed Policy or Non-Payment

If your policy lapsed due to non-payment of premiums before the loss occurred, the claim will be denied. Check your policy status and payment history.

## Common Reason 3: Late Reporting

Most policies require you to report claims within a specific time period. Waiting too long to report can result in denial based on prejudice to the insurer's investigation.

## Common Reason 4: Insufficient Documentation

Insurers deny claims when documentation is incomplete or does not support the amount claimed. Providing thorough documentation — receipts, photos, medical records, police reports — reduces this risk.

## Common Reason 5: Disputed Liability or Cause of Loss

The insurer may dispute that the loss was caused by a covered event, or that you were not at fault. They may argue the damage was caused by wear and tear, a pre-existing condition, or another excluded cause.

## Common Reason 6: Material Misrepresentation

If the insurer finds that you provided inaccurate information when applying for the policy or filing the claim, they may deny the claim for misrepresentation.

## What to Do After a Denial

Request the denial in writing with the specific reason. Review your policy against the stated reason. File an internal appeal if you believe the denial is incorrect. If the appeal fails, consider filing a complaint with your state insurance commissioner or consulting an attorney.
