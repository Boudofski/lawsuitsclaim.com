## Short Answer

An insurance claim appeal is a formal request asking your insurance company to reconsider a claim denial or an underpayment. Most insurers have a defined internal appeal process, and external options exist if the internal appeal fails.

## Why Appeals Matter

Claim denials are not final until you exhaust your appeal rights. A significant percentage of denials that are appealed are reversed or result in increased payments. Many policyholders accept denials without appealing, unaware that the process is available and often effective.

## Types of Insurance Appeals

Internal appeal: A formal review by the insurance company's own appeals department — different from the original adjuster. This is the first step.

External review: For health insurance claims, federal law gives you the right to an independent external review by a third party after you exhaust internal appeals. Some states offer external review for other insurance types.

Arbitration: Some policies require arbitration for certain disputes rather than litigation.

Regulatory complaint: Filing a complaint with your state insurance department is not technically an appeal but can prompt the insurer to reconsider.

## What Makes an Effective Appeal

Address the specific reason for denial point by point. Include supporting documentation — medical records, repair estimates, photographs, professional opinions, policy language. Cite the exact policy provisions you believe support coverage. Submit within the appeal deadline stated in your denial letter.

## Appeal Deadlines

Appeal deadlines vary by policy and state. For health insurance, the ACA requires at least 180 days to file an internal appeal. For property and auto claims, policy deadlines vary — read your policy or ask your adjuster.

## What to Do If Your Appeal Is Denied

File a complaint with your state insurance department. Consult an insurance attorney who handles bad faith claims. Consider arbitration or litigation depending on the amount and the policy terms.
