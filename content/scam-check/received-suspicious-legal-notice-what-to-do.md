## Short Answer

When you receive a suspicious legal notice, do not ignore it and do not immediately comply. Instead, independently verify whether the case, company, or settlement mentioned is real before providing any information or payment.

## Step 1: Read It Carefully

Note the case name, case number, court name, and any attorney or settlement administrator names. These details are verifiable — and their absence is itself a red flag.

## Step 2: Search for the Case Independently

Use a search engine to look up the case name plus the court name. For federal cases, search PACER at pacer.gov. For state cases, search your state court's online public records.

## Step 3: Verify the Sender

If the notice claims to be from a law firm or settlement administrator, search for that firm independently and use contact information you find on your own, not what is in the letter. Call and ask if the notice is legitimate.

## Step 4: Look for Red Flags

The notice asks for payment. There is no verifiable case number or court name. The deadline is extremely short, creating pressure. The language is threatening or sensational. The return address or website does not match the law firm or administrator's verified information.

## Step 5: Consult an Attorney If Warranted

If the notice claims you are being sued, involves a court summons or subpoena, or threatens serious legal consequences you do not understand, consult a licensed attorney promptly. Do not ignore a legitimate summons — failing to respond to a real lawsuit has serious consequences.

## What Not to Do

Do not pay money in response to an unverified notice. Do not provide personal information including your Social Security number or financial account details. Do not click links in the notice without independently verifying the URL.
