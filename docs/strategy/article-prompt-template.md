## Prompt Template

Write a [1,200–2,200 word / 2,500–4,000 word cornerstone] article for LawsuitsClaim.com on the following topic:

**Title:** [INSERT TITLE]
**Primary keyword:** [INSERT KEYWORD]
**Secondary keywords:** [INSERT LIST]
**Category:** [INSERT CATEGORY]
**Reader problem:** [INSERT: what specific problem the reader is trying to solve]

**Structure requirements — include all of these sections:**

1. **Short Answer** (2–3 sentences at top, directly answering the primary keyword intent)
2. **Main explanation** (break into 4–6 subheadings using H2/H3, plain English, no jargon without explanation)
3. **Step-by-step or numbered process** (where applicable)
4. **Example scenario** (concrete, realistic, US-based)
5. **Common Mistakes** (3–5 specific mistakes readers make)
6. **Red Flags** (where applicable — warning signs relevant to the topic)
7. **When to Contact a Lawyer** (only where genuinely relevant; do not manufacture this section)
8. **Sources to Verify** (list 2–4 official sources: FTC, CFPB, PACER, state AG, CPSC, etc.)
9. **FAQ** (3–5 questions and concise answers in plain English)
10. **Legal disclaimer** (at bottom: "This article is for general informational purposes only and is not legal advice. Laws vary by jurisdiction. Consult a licensed attorney for advice specific to your situation.")

**Critical content rules:**

- Write in plain English. Explain every legal term you use.
- Do NOT say readers "qualify for" money unless quoting a specific, verifiable eligibility rule from an official source.
- Do NOT promise compensation or suggest a specific dollar amount.
- Do NOT create urgency that is not backed by a real, documented deadline.
- Do NOT copy text from settlement administrator websites.
- Do NOT invent attorney names, bar numbers, law firm names, or legal credentials.
- Do NOT give legal advice. Explain the process, not what the reader should do in their specific case.
- Avoid fearmongering. Avoid sensational language.
- Include at least one internal link suggestion to a related LawsuitsClaim article (note as: [INTERNAL LINK: /category/slug]).
- Avoid AI-sounding phrases like "delve into," "it's important to note," "in conclusion."

**Tone:** Clear, calm, serious, helpful, non-sensational. The tone of a knowledgeable friend explaining something complex — not a lawyer or a salesperson.

**Audience:** U.S. adult who received a legal notice, experienced an injury, or has a consumer problem and wants to understand what it means before deciding what to do.
